Foundation & Overview

CPT FOUNDATION — SESSIONS 1–10

Communication • Obedience • Drive • Leash Skills
This program is built as a 10-session coaching system, not a rigid 10-week course. Each session introduces a new skill and owners typically spend 1–3 weeks practising before progressing. That allows the dog to develop confidence, the handler to improve timing and communication, and the coach to assess progress based on real-world results.
Individual Dog Suitability
Every dog is assessed before training is planned. We consider the dog's age, temperament, stress levels, handler skills and current triggers.
Stability • Recovery • Welfare
The value of the work is not only in obedience; it is in creating emotional stability, recovery capacity and safe, humane handling.
Handler Control
Dogs are taught to respond to calm, clear cues. This is a communication system, not a dominance model.
Advanced Training
This foundation is a base for advanced obedience, neutrality, controlled drive and higher-level public reliability.

How the foundation connects to advanced protection training

The foundation is not a separate system from advanced work. It is the framework that creates the dog's willingness to engage, recover, remain stable and follow clear handler direction under pressure. Without these fundamentals, advanced work becomes inconsistent and unsafe.

CPT Advanced Training — Optional

Advanced obedience, neutrality, controlled drive, and structured assessment may be added after the foundation is stable. This stage is not required for every dog and is tailored to suitability and welfare.

The 5 CPT Classical Pillars

Pillar Core Mechanism Application
1. Movement S-Flow, leash skills, following the handler’s body language and directional changes. Builds control, balance and movement around the handler in daily life and in public environments.
2. Visuals Source focus, voluntary engagement and the ability to switch attention when asked. Develops calm attention to the handler and reward source without forcing eye contact.
3. Vocals Commands, reward markers, release markers and reinforcement timing. Creates clear communication between dog and handler. Calm verbal communication is essential.
4. Drive Food motivation, toy play, enthusiasm and the ability to switch between tasks calmly. Builds engagement without creating chaos. High motivation is useful when structured correctly.
5. Grip Optional toy engagement with confident possession and voluntary release. Not protection or bite-work. It is a confidence and control skill where appropriate.

CPT CANINE
Sessions 1–4

PHASE 1: BUILDING MOTIVATION & CONTROL

Session 1 Drive
Building Food Drive
Make food reinforcement valuable and teach the dog that interaction with the handler leads to reward, clarity and opportunity.
  • Hand-feeding using the measured daily ration.
  • Following the food hand in multiple directions.
  • Food chase, food magnet and marker conditioning.
Homework: 2–3 short sessions dailyGoal: motivation & engagement
Session 2 Visuals + Vocals
Source Focus & Break
Build focus on the reward source and teach Break as a transition cue between tasks, not an emergency interruption.
  • Source focus on the active hand or reward source.
  • Switching between reward sources and attention.
  • Break training and re-engagement into simple work.
Homework: 3–5 minutes twice dailyGoal: clarity & transitions
Session 3 Movement
Movement Foundations
Teach body control, position changes, and connection through circles, S-flow and contact heel.
  • Sit, Down and Stand using gentle lure work.
  • Left and right circles with reward-based movement.
  • S-Flow path work and contact heel introduction.
Homework: two 5-minute sessions dailyGoal: smooth movement & position
Session 4 Movement + Duration
Fading Lures, Duration & Leash Skills
Reduce dependence on visible food, create calm duration and begin loose-leash control without force.
  • Empty-hand lure work and early verbal cueing.
  • Short duration at Sit and Down.
  • Leash pressure yielding and loose-leash walking.
Homework: 5–8 minutes twice dailyGoal: duration & leash control

Sessions 5–10

PHASE 2: DRIVE, RECALL & REAL-WORLD RELIABILITY

Session 5 Drive + Grip
Ball Drive, Positive Out & Leash Integration
Introduce structured play, controlled toy release and calm movement after excitement.
  • Toy activation and short chase games.
  • Positive Out training using voluntary release.
  • Transition from play back to handler direction.
Homework: one or two play sessions dailyGoal: controlled enthusiasm
Session 6 Distraction
Positions Under Distractions
Teach the dog that known behaviours remain intact when the environment changes.
  • Three-location practice indoors and outdoors.
  • Duration with movement and mild visual distractions.
  • Distance and object-based interruption work.
Homework: 5–10 minutes dailyGoal: calm response under change
Session 7 Recall
Recall Foundations
Build a reliable response to the recall cue using reward history and clarity rather than punishment.
  • Name response and short directional recalls.
  • Recall between handlers and moving recall.
  • Long-line practice in safe, open areas.
Homework: 5–10 successful recalls dailyGoal: reliable return
Session 8 Emergency Recall
Super Recall / Emergency Recall
Create a distinct, higher-value emergency response for situations where instant return matters most.
  • Choose a unique recall cue or sound pattern.
  • Charge the cue with multiple rewards and short success.
  • Use only in safe, controlled scenarios.
Homework: 3–5 planned repetitions weeklyGoal: emergency response
Session 9 Impulse Control
Impulse Control & Real-Life Obedience
Combine all skills into useful household and public behaviours: waiting, calm transitions, and responsive movement.
  • Doorway waiting and recall sequences.
  • Mat work and Leave It exercises.
  • Short real-life sequences with calm recovery.
Homework: 3–5 minute practice blocks dailyGoal: everyday control
Session 10 Final Assessment
Bunnings Real-World Assessment
A practical evaluation of the dog's training and the owner's ability to manage calm communication in a real public setting.
  • Calm arrival and engagement at store entry.
  • Loose-leash movement, stationary positions and distraction control.
  • Recovery, recall and calm exit, based on suitability.
Homework: final public practice reviewGoal: practical reliability

Method & Standards

COACHING PHILOSOPHY

CPT Promise
CPT Canine Personal Training is about building communication, motivation and control. We do not just train the dog; we teach the owner how to become a better handler. The foundation is designed to create steady, calm dogs that can work with confidence and recover without confusion.

1. Say it once, guide smoothly. Never repeat a verbal cue unnecessarily. Clear body language and timing matter more than volume.

2. Assess both ends of the leash. If a behaviour fails, review both the dog's current capacity and the handler's mechanics.

3. Stay calm. A relaxed handler produces a more confident and responsive dog.

4. Train the real environment. Practice should match the world the dog actually lives in.

5. Distinguish the cues clearly. Break means transition away from the current activity; Free releases a position; Out means release a toy or object.

Owner Homework & Progress Tracking
A one-page homework sheet should be issued for every session with the exercise instructions, practice target, short video reference and a simple progress assessment. This creates accountability, helps the dog learn faster, and allows the coach to review real consistency between lessons.
